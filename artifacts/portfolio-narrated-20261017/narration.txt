Your memories shouldn’t need a form. OpenMemory turns a simple thought into something you can return to.

Start with: I want to read Sapiens. That’s a book you plan to read, not a book you’ve finished. Both the subject and the action matter.

On this Mac, a custom, fine-tuned model classifies the note using Apple’s M L X framework. A separate local model extracts the title and prepares the review.

These are actual app captures. Processing waits are shortened here; this is not a speed benchmark.

The review shows Sapiens, Book, and something I want to do. You can check the original words and edit the details. Nothing is saved until you confirm.

Choose Save, and the book appears on your wishlist. In this run, the item also survived a reload. Your memories stay in this browser’s vault.

The adapter was trained on seven hundred and thirty examples. On a two hundred note test, it improved over its base from sixty-seven to seventy-six point five percent joint accuracy, but stayed below the eighty-one percent majority baseline. It remains experimental.

The application, research code, and model weights are available to inspect. OpenMemory combines local fine-tuning with a simple principle: A I suggests. You decide what becomes a memory.
